        How to play

        Every row, every column and every coloured region needs exactly one queen, and no two queens may touch, not even corner to corner.

        Tap a square once to cross it out, again to place a queen, and a third time to clear it. Drag across squares to cross out a run of them. Crosses are notes to yourself: the puzzle is solved the moment the last queen lands in the right place, whatever else is marked.

        Each board has exactly one solution, and it can always be reached by reasoning alone. You never need to guess.

        Controls

        Keyboard: arrow keys move, Space cycles a square, X crosses, Q places a queen, Backspace clears, Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Shift+Z undo and redo.

        Sheets

        A sheet key such as 10-K7Q2MX names a puzzle: the number is the board size and the rest is a code. The key seeds every random choice the generator makes, so the same key always draws the same board, with the same regions and colours, on any device. The address carries the key after a #, so a bookmark or a shared link reopens that board.

        Keys ignore case and punctuation, and any word will do: 12-gloucester is as good a key as a random one. A key without a size uses the size currently selected.

        The solver

        The Solver tab works through a sheet the way a person would: one deduction at a time, always the easiest one available, and never a guess. Each step first outlines the rows, columns or regions it reasons about and shows in teal where it will act; then it places the queen or the crosses. A new queen's crosses ripple outward from it.

        Play runs the solve and the slider sets its speed. Step, Back, Reset and End move one step or jump to either end. The strip under the board has a bar for every step, taller for harder deductions; click one to replay it. Your board, as it stands continues from your marks on the Play tab, as long as none of them is wrong.

        Watching the sheet you're playing counts as help, so solving it afterwards won't set a best time. Watch another sheet draws a different board there without touching your game. In a terminal, node tools/solve.mjs 10-K7Q2MX prints the same step-by-step solution.

        Shortcuts

        A shortcut is a pattern worth recognising on sight. Offset dominoes is one: when two rows, columns or regions are each down to two side-by-side squares, in neighbouring lines and shifted by one, both queens go on the two outer squares, because a queen on either inner square would touch both squares of the other pair.

        The solver itself knows only four general techniques. Each step it takes is matched to the most specific shortcut that fully explains it, and a shortcut joins your collection the first time you see it used, by the solver or in a hint. Each card shows the pattern, says how often you've seen it, and Show me replays its first appearance. The collection is kept in this browser.

        Training

        Getting faster at Queens is mostly about seeing a handful of patterns sooner. Across a thousand boards, the hardest step usually comes early, and once the last one falls about three quarters of the queens follow mechanically. The Training tab practises those moments directly instead of whole puzzles.

        Spot the key step
        A real position just before a deduction that decides the board. Tap any square you can rule out; the clock stops on the first correct square, and the deduction is shown. Drill one named skill and only its squares count: a square some other deduction rules out gets a nudge, not a penalty.
        Opening sweep
        Find every region that sits in one row or column. On almost every board, that's the first move.
        Three-move trial
        At a hard impasse, pick which of two squares holds a queen. The wrong branch is then played out, move by move, until something runs out of squares.
        Cascade
        Place the forced queens at the end of a solve as fast as you can.
        Mixed
        Picks the skill you're weakest at, never the same twice running, and adds Hard and Expert skills as your Medium ones improve.

        Each skill opens with a worked example before its first timed attempt. After a solve on the Play tab, the review shows how long each queen took and, for the slowest, the deduction that would have unlocked it, with a link to practise that skill. Records are kept in this browser.

        How each puzzle is made

        The answer comes first. The generator places the queens at random, never sharing a row or column and never touching, then grows one region outward from each queen. The answer is now certain to be valid, but it is almost never the only one: a fresh 14 × 14 layout typically admits thousands of others.

        An exhaustive search then finds those rival answers. Any square where a rival puts a queen and the real answer doesn't can be handed to a neighbouring region, which kills every rival using that square and leaves the real answer intact. The generator repeats this, always aiming at the squares most rivals share, until the search proves the answer is the only one.

        Finally a second solver works the board the way a person would, using only the deductions listed under Difficulty. If it stalls, the generator reshapes a region at the sticking point and checks everything again. A board is shown only once it has a single answer that reasoning alone can reach. All of this runs in your browser, usually in a fraction of a second.

        Difficulty

        The label beside the sheet key names the hardest deduction the board needs. A square is available until it is crossed out or a queen rules it out.

        Easy
        A row, column or region has one available square left; or all of a region's available squares lie in one row or column, so the rest of that line can be crossed out.
        Medium
        A queen on some square would leave a row, column or region with nowhere to go, so that square can be crossed out.
        Hard
        Two regions fit entirely inside the same two rows or columns (or the reverse), so nothing else can use them.
        Expert
        The same, with three.

        What's exact and what isn't

        Exact: every board has one solution, confirmed by exhaustive search; the deductions above are enough to reach it; and a sheet key always gives the same board.

        Judgement calls: the difficulty label counts techniques, not minutes, so a large Easy board can still take a while. Regions are kept between two squares and three times the board's width, so none is a free queen and none swallows the board. Best times and solve counts are stored in this browser only.
